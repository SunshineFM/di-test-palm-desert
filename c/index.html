Palm Desert weighs a citywide AI policy for staff and officials

Thursday's council agenda includes a risk-tiered framework that replaces interim guidance and gives the city's AI officer an administrative role.

The Palm Desert City Council is scheduled to consider a citywide artificial intelligence policy at its regular meeting at 4 p.m. Thursday, Oct. 8. If the council approves it, the policy takes effect immediately.

The policy would replace interim guidance that focused mainly on generative AI during the city's early experimentation. City officials drafted the broader framework after creating an Artificial Intelligence Officer position in March 2026. That officer would administer the policy and the city's AI governance and risk classification program.

The policy sorts AI uses into three risk tiers. Routine use of city-approved tools would be allowed for city employees, a group that includes interns, volunteers, and elected and appointed officials. Moderate- and high-risk uses need sign-off from a department director or the city manager. According to the staff report, those uses include accessing sensitive or restricted city information, communicating or acting independently without prior human approval, and taking an autonomous action that creates a legal or financial commitment for the city.

The policy also bars using AI to fabricate or falsify a city record or communication, to falsely attribute a statement or action to a real person or to the city, or to get around city security controls and required approvals.

Patch reported that the framework centers on human accountability, transparent public interactions and a risk-based approval process.
